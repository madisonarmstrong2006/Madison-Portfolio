Most fashion brands think about the customer up to the moment of purchase. For my Parsons Fashion Business Essentials capstone, I wanted to design one that keeps thinking about the garment long after it leaves the store.

The result is Mischief Maison Studio, a sustainable womenswear concept built around a simple question: what happens to a piece of clothing when its first owner is ready to let it go?

## The problem on both sides of the closet

Fast fashion is taking over, and it leaves lasting environmental effects through unsustainable products and a lot of waste. At the same time, many women are looking for the opposite: ethical products, sustainability, and timeless style rather than trend-based garments.

There is a quieter problem too. Women who cannot afford to dress professionally face fewer opportunities when they try to enter the workforce, and access to business-style clothing should not decide how anyone is evaluated.

> Our mission is to build a community of stylish women who support creativity, more equal opportunities for other women, and a more sustainable future.

## Reimagine or redirect

The model gives every pre-loved Mischief Maison garment one of two second lives.

**Reimagine.** Pieces are taken back and reworked into limited-edition, one-of-a-kind designs:

- Fabrics become patchwork, panels, color blocks, and new silhouettes
- Buttons and zippers are reused
- Garments are transformed into accessory pieces
- Leftover fabric turns into bows, pockets, trims, and other details

**Redirect.** Pieces go to partner organizations that support women entering the workforce, who lend business attire for job interviews and key moments, then return it so the next woman has the same opportunity.

Customers earn store credit for every pre-loved item they bring back, which keeps the product line fresh, mysterious, and exclusive while building a community around it.

## Who it is for

The target customer is an urban, career-driven woman somewhere between her mid-twenties and forty. She values quality over quantity, supports women's empowerment and philanthropy, and wants pieces that move easily between streetwear, business casual, and formal.

That is also why the price point is higher. You are not only buying a garment, you are joining a community that is always contributing to a greater purpose.

## Does the math work?

A concept is only convincing if the numbers hold up, so the capstone includes a three-year forecast built on a $150,000 funding request, split across marketing, e-commerce and operations, employees, and working capital.

The forecast shows a first-year loss of $27,000, then a profit of $59,950 in year two and $182,150 in year three, as order volume grows from 750 to 2,750 a year.
