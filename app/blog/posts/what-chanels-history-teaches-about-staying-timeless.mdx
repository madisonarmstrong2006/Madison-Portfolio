Chanel is one of the few fashion houses that feels both old and current at once. When I researched its history for a brand timeline, I wanted to understand how that is possible. What I found is that staying timeless is not about standing still.

## 1909: a founder who made her own way

Coco Chanel learned to sew in an orphanage and started creating her own women's accessories. She opened a millinery shop in Paris, and a brand that would outlive her was born.

## The 1920s: a signature scent and a new silhouette

Chanel No. 5 was created as an addition to complement the accessories she was already selling, in partnership with the perfumer Ernest Beaux. At the same time, boyish silhouettes were trending, and Chanel embraced them, choosing colors associated with masculinity.

## World War II: a pause, then a return

The business shut down for a while. Coco was heavily criticized for her involvement with the Nazi intelligence services, and thousands of employees lost their jobs. When she returned to work after the war, she continued to grow the brand and embraced the feminine new look of the era, which is when her staple pearl earrings were born.

## The 1970s and 1980s: new leaders, same essence

After Coco Chanel died, Alain Wertheimer took over, and the brand moved from couture to worldwide ready-to-wear products. In the 1980s, Karl Lagerfeld became chief designer and made sure the classic, timeless essence stayed intact and the brand stayed exclusive.

That consistency is the point. Customers know what to expect from Chanel, and that trust is a large part of its value.

## The 2000s: a bold decision

Chanel banned fur and exotic skins from its collections. It was a bold choice, but one the house felt passionate about, and it shows that a heritage brand can update its values without losing its look.

## What it teaches

- Keep one clear point of view, and let everything else change around it
- Let each new generation of leadership reinterpret the brand rather than replace it
- Adjust to the values of the moment when it matters, and be prepared to stand behind the decision
